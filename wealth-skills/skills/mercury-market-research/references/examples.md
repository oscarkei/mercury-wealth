# Market research examples

## A why follow-up

Sequence: "Compare NVDA, META and AMD over six months" → "Why?"

Restore the same assets, exact dates and price evidence. Retrieve dated company evidence where supported. Explain each company's documented developments and then a conditional economic mechanism. Do not replace the universe with a generic technology basket or simply repeat price returns.

Response pattern: "The divergence is consistent with different operating developments across the companies. [Reported company evidence.] One plausible transmission channel is [clearly labeled inference]. This evidence supports an interpretation, rather than proving the cause of the share-price move."

## No consensus

Reported quarterly revenue rises, but no consensus dataset is supplied. Say revenue increased with an issuer/period-specific metric reference. Do not call it an earnings beat. Sources must identify the issuer and period, not merely contain the same number.

## Partial history

One ticker lacks overlapping observations. Keep the supported comparison, mark the missing asset, and avoid ranking disjoint windows as if comparable. A stale cached result cannot silently answer a request for a refreshed analysis.

## Explicit historical cutoff

User asks what was knowable at 2026-06-30. Exclude later publication dates even if the document's fiscal period ends earlier. Preserve the cutoff on the next "why" or "what about my holdings" turn.
